# Marathon Planning Guide

> Added for the workshop repo (the codelab references this file but does not include it).
> Figures are common rules of thumb for planning exercises, not official certification rules.

## Distance

- Official marathon distance: **42.195 km / 26.2 miles** (26 miles 385 yards).
- Certified courses are measured on the shortest possible path a runner can take.
- Courses usually add a small safety margin (about 0.1%) so they are never short.

## Road width

| Participants     | Minimum road width at the start | Along the course |
|------------------|---------------------------------|------------------|
| < 5,000          | 2 lanes (~7 m)                  | 1–2 lanes        |
| 5,000 – 20,000   | 4 lanes (~14 m)                 | 2 lanes          |
| > 20,000         | 6+ lanes, wave starts required  | 2–3 lanes        |

## Traffic severity (closure impact)

- **Low**: residential streets, park roads, closed on a Sunday morning.
- **Medium**: commercial arterials; needs detour signage and advance notice.
- **High**: highways, bridges, hospital access roads; needs emergency crossings every ~2 miles (3 km) and police coordination.

## Course services

- Water stations: about every 5 km (3 miles), both sides of the road for large fields.
- Medical: first-aid point roughly every 8 km plus a large medical tent at the finish; ambulances staged near high-risk segments (late miles, hills, heat).
- Timing: chip timing with mats at start, finish and every 5 km split.
- Course marshals: at every turn and intersection; higher density in the last 10 km.
- Portable toilets: ~1 per 100 runners at the start, plus units at every water station.

## Route design heuristics

- Start and finish close together (loop or near-loop) simplifies logistics.
- Put landmarks in the middle miles to lift runners when fatigue starts.
- Avoid long uphill segments after 30 km for a "fast" theme.
- Cheer zones in dense neighborhoods improve community buy-in.
